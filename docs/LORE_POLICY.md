# Lore and canon policy

## Selected continuity

The authoritative continuity is the [Backrooms Wiki on Fandom](https://backrooms.fandom.com/wiki/Backrooms_Wiki). Do not silently merge Wikidot, Kane Pixels, other fan projects, or personal interpretations into this continuity.

## Evidence requirements

Every shipped level, entity, object, group, phenomenon, and transition must cite an individual source page. For a connection, cite the page documenting the entrance/exit behavior and destination. A general overview is insufficient if it does not substantiate the specific connection.

Record the exact page URL/title, canonical name and classification, source-supported description, transition direction and destination, source-supported conditions/exceptions/rarity, review date, and implementation notes.

## Connection scope

Each implemented level receives one or two selected documented entrances and one or two selected documented exits. This is an implementation limit, not a claim that the wiki documents only that many. Prefer recognizable, playable, well-supported connections. Do not invent shortcuts, destinations, conditions, or probabilities. Keep ambiguous or disputed connections out of release data until reviewed.

## Classification and adaptation

Keep numbered levels, enigmatic levels, sublevels, rooms, objects, entities, groups, and phenomena in their proper categories. Mark uncertain, archived, deleted, joke, and unusual entries explicitly. Minecraft-specific mechanics may be original only when they do not contradict source lore; label them as gameplay adaptation.

## Review checklist

1. Review the individual Fandom page.
2. Confirm current title, classification, and relevant claims.
3. Verify each chosen transition from source text.
4. Verify the destination is documented.
5. Record uncertainty rather than filling gaps with assumptions.
6. Run validation and tests for special conditions.
